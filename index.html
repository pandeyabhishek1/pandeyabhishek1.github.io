Welcome

I am a PhD student in Econometrics and Quantitative Economics at The University of Texas at Dallas.

My research interest are in the intersection of applied econometrics, causal inference, and health economics. I am primarily interested in heterogenous treatment effects evaluation using distributional and quantile treatment effects estimators. My research studies how to estimate and diagnose treatment effects across the outcome distribution, and how public policies affect people beyond their intended targets.

I teach undergraduate econometrics and mathematical economics, and have supported economics courses at both the undergraduate and doctoral levels.

Working papers & ongoing work

Distributional Weak σ-Convergence and Quantile Treatment Effects

With Donggyu Sul · May 2026· Working paper

A diagnostic framework for assessing the stability of control-group distributions before estimating quantile treatment effects, with a sieving procedure to construct a non-divergent control group.

Abstract

This paper proposes a pre-estimation diagnostic for quantile treatment effects on the treated. Weak and distributional σ-convergence tests assess whether the control distribution remains non-divergent around its mean and relevant quantiles. When it fails these diagnostics, a sieving algorithm constructs a distributionally non-divergent control group. The procedure then tests whether the treated distribution changes only by a common location shift or whether treated-control quantile gaps diverge over time, informing the choice between average and quantile treatment-effect analysis.

Quantile and Distributional Difference-in-Differences: A Survey and Practitioner’s Guide

August 2026 · Working paper

A guide to identifying, estimating, and diagnosing distributional treatment effects, organized around the assumptions used to construct the missing counterfactual distribution.

Abstract

This survey follows quantile and distributional difference-in-differences methods from their economic arguments to estimation, diagnostics, and inference. It covers changes-in-changes, distributional parallel trends, panel methods, staggered designs, and partial identification. A running application and an assumption-focused Monte Carlo illustrate why agreement between models is not identification and why resampling must follow treatment assignment. The practical emphasis is on defining the distributional target, defending the counterfactual restriction, and reporting sensitivity or bounds when no point-identifying method is credible.

Local Immigration Enforcement and Medicare: Health-System Spillovers from County Sanctuary Policies

September 2026 · Working paper

Evidence on how county sanctuary policies affect Medicare spending and inpatient utilization, linking local enforcement changes to health-system outcomes.

Abstract

This chapter links sanctuary-policy dates for 296 large U.S. counties to Medicare records from 2007–2024 and administrative immigration-detainer data. Sanctuary adoption disrupts the local jail-to-ICE pipeline, followed by delayed increases in Medicare spending and inpatient utilization. The evidence supports a health-system spillover following a change in local enforcement cooperation, but does not determine whether additional care reflects access, accumulated health needs, provider behavior, coding, or beneficiary composition.
